Courtyard Tree Planner

Enter a courtyard and a tree type. The planner draws a layout, shows how each arrangement performed in my M.Sc. ENVI-met simulations, and estimates the irrigation water the trees would need.

Prototype, not a prediction. Cooling figures are ENVI-met simulation results for one hot summer day in Dehradun, India (subtropical), at one point per courtyard. They show what the PhD will test for Saudi courtyards; they are not results for your courtyard. Every number is labelled with where it comes from.

Design notes for this courtyard

    Irrigation estimate

    Landscape coefficient method

    Water need per tree = species factor × microclimate factor × ET₀ × crown area ÷ irrigation efficiency (Costello & Jones, WUCOLS). Crown area is the circle of the model's crown spread. Default ET₀ of 9.5 mm/day is an approximate regional value; summer peaks in Riyadh are higher, so enter local data.

    What the PhD will add

    PhD
    Any courtyard, not three patternsENVI-met runs across many sizes, heights and orientations, with a machine-learning model that predicts comfort in seconds.
    Saudi climateRiyadh and Jeddah weather, arid-adapted species such as Sidr and Ghaf, validated against local observations.
    Water as a design goalIrrigation regimes modelled directly, so the planner can find the most cooling per cubic metre of water.
    Life-cycle footprintCarbon and water footprint of the water source (desalinated, treated wastewater, groundwater) over the trees' life.
